For employers

Starting an engagement

An engagement begins with a written scope and a service agreement, not a sales process. This page describes what Foundling needs from you, what you keep control of, and exactly how billing works.

How to start

  1. Send the scope

    Email hello@foundlingllc.com with the roles, headcount, worksite location, schedules, and target pay rates. A short description is enough to begin.

  2. Review the proposal

    Foundling responds with a proposed bill rate per role and a draft service agreement covering responsibilities, terms, and conversion provisions for temp to hire roles.

  3. Sign and onboard

    Once the agreement is signed, Foundling recruits or transitions the workers, completes onboarding documentation, and coordinates start dates with your site supervisor.

  4. Run the work

    Your supervisors direct the work and approve time each pay period. Foundling runs payroll and sends one itemized invoice.

What Foundling needs from a client

  • Role descriptions with pay rates and schedules.
  • Worksite address and a named supervisor contact for each site.
  • Site-specific safety requirements and any training the role requires.
  • Approved time records each pay period, on the schedule set in the agreement.
  • Prompt notice of performance issues, schedule changes, or ending an assignment.

What the client keeps control of

The employer of record structure moves employment administration to Foundling. It does not move your operation:

You control
What work is done, how it is done, schedules, worksite standards, equipment, and whether a worker's assignment at your site continues.
Foundling controls
Hiring paperwork, payroll, tax filings, insurance coverage, wage and hour records, and the formal employment relationship, including any termination of employment.
Shared
Worksite safety. You maintain the site and its conditions; Foundling carries the workers compensation coverage and needs incidents reported promptly.

How billing works

Each role has a bill rate, set in the service agreement. For hourly roles it is billed per hour worked; for salaried roles, per pay period. The bill rate is the whole cost. It covers:

  • Gross wages paid to the worker
  • Employer payroll taxes
  • Workers compensation and New York State disability coverage
  • Foundling's administration fee

Invoices are itemized by worker, with hours and rates shown, and issued on the cycle set in the agreement. There are no placement fees hidden inside the rate and no charges that are not in the agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the legal employer?

Foundling LLC. It hires the worker, withholds and files employment taxes, and carries workers compensation and disability coverage. Your company is the client, not the employer.

Can we hire a placed worker onto our own payroll?

Yes. Temp to hire conversion is a normal outcome, and the terms for conversion are set in the service agreement before anyone starts.

Who handles unemployment and workers compensation claims?

Foundling, as the employer of record. You provide facts about the assignment when a claim needs them.

What happens if a worker is not working out?

You end the assignment at your site and tell Foundling. Foundling handles the employment side, including reassignment or separation.

Can Foundling take over workers we already have?

Yes. Existing workers can be transitioned onto Foundling's payroll through the same onboarding process used for new hires.

What does an engagement cost?

The bill rate per role, stated in the proposal before you sign. Rates depend on the wage, the role's insurance classification, and the scope of administration, so they are quoted per engagement rather than published.
